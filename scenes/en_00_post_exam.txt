Behind you, stone begins to move, slow and heavy, until the passage to the final examination chamber is completely sealed. For a moment, only the silence of the corridor remains.

*page_break Continue

Then you notice Corvus. He is waiting a few steps away, his hands resting calmly behind his back. His hair has long since turned completely grey, and the years have left fine lines around his eyes and across his brow. Yet there is nothing frail in the way he carries himself. His gaze is as alert and steady as you remember it – attentive in a way that can sometimes make it seem as though he notices a question before anyone has spoken it aloud.

Corvus is one of those who have accompanied your training in Ithar’kael time and again. Never as a personal mentor guiding your every step, but often enough that you know his brief remarks almost as well as the pauses that precede them. An answer rarely satisfied him when a better question could still be found behind it.

Today, there is no further question waiting for you. At least, not the kind written on an examination page. Corvus watches you not like someone searching for a mistake, but like someone who has been waiting for something that is now complete.

“You passed.” The words come without ceremony, almost matter-of-factly. Corvus lets a moment pass. “But that was never all this was about.” His gaze briefly shifts to the closed door behind you. “The examination was never meant to prescribe an answer. It was meant to show how you search, what you do once you find an answer—and what you do when knowledge suddenly becomes tangible.” He looks back at you. “In the first section, you could only move forward by truly paying attention. Not every clue was an answer, and not everything visible was important. In the second section, you already had knowledge. But knowledge alone was not enough. You had to use it, make connections, and determine what could actually lead you forward.” His gaze returns once more to the sealed chamber. “And in the final section, you could no longer simply observe. You became part of the answer. Your magic. Your questions. Your understanding of what knowledge means.”

Corvus lets the silence remain between you for a moment. “Today, you showed how you search. You said what you believe about knowledge. And you acted when it became personal.” A brief pause. “Ithar’kael has set itself the task of seeking every truth, no matter how deeply hidden or obscured it may be. We follow myths until we understand what lies behind them. We gather traces, compare accounts, examine evidence, and question even what has long been accepted as certainty.” His gaze rests steadily on you. “But finding a truth is only the beginning.”

“In the end, we always have to decide what to do with what we have learned. Do we leave an ancient artifact where we found it because that place may be part of its history, its purpose, or its protection? Or do we take it with us because we believe knowledge should be preserved, studied, or made accessible to others? And what changes simply because we make that choice?” Corvus exhales slowly.

“The irony of our work is that we can rarely know with absolute certainty whether a decision was right or wrong. We can only weigh what we know. Question it. Take responsibility. And try to think not only about what we want to know, but also about what our knowledge may mean for others.” For a moment, something almost ceremonial enters his voice. “Truth is no mere opinion. It is a mystery that reveals itself only to those willing to question everything—even themselves.”

Something in Corvus’ posture changes, barely perceptible, yet enough for you to know that the examination is truly over. “Your final examination is complete.” He steps aside, leaving the corridor open before you. “I expect you in the Great Hall later.” His gaze remains on you for another moment. “Until then, your time is your own.”

You have already begun to walk past him when his voice reaches you once more. “Seek what is hidden. Question what is known.”
