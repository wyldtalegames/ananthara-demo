[b]WHAT WE CARRY WITHIN[/b]

The passage leads into a vast circular chamber. Unlike the two sections before it, there is no distinct source of light here. A faint glow lies across the stone—just enough to distinguish shapes, edges and depictions from one another.

At the centre stands a stone pedestal. Its surface is empty except for fine golden lines depicting an open book. You recognize the same shape that emerged from the hidden network of light at the end of the second section. You still do not know what book it depicts or what it means.

Mineral channels lead from the golden book into the pedestal's base, through the floor and onward to three immense murals. A broad stone pressure plate lies before each image. Low plinths carry the same crystalline structures. Everything appears complete and functional.

Yet everything remains dark.

What do you want to do?

*label carry_light
You step upon the first pressure plate and focus your Thal’ithara into the exposed channel. Its appearance bears the unmistakable language of your soul, but the mechanism remains the same.

Your energy reaches the first mural and awakens between the open hands of the Three. From there it runs through the young world. One line becomes two; two become many. Your Thal’ithara takes the place of the light within the depiction.

At the outer edge, the glow stops. The connection to the second image lies open, but creating light alone is not enough.

What is given must be carried forward.

You hold the connection and guide the same energy onward.

In the second image, your Thal’ithara moves through roots, buildings, tools, works of art and hands. Lines divide and meet again. It remains unmistakably your magic, yet its passage leaves traces. What is passed onward does not remain wholly unchanged. It assumes new paths and new forms.

At last it reaches the connection to the third mural.

The third plate opens the damaged plinth. The surviving golden lines begin to glow and lead directly into the blackened surface. Every visible path disappears there.

You remember the second section. Paths of light vanished beneath the veil's dark pigment and emerged later. Invisible did not mean ended.

You carry your Thal’ithara onward.

The light enters the black surface.

And disappears.

The connection remains. You can still feel your magic though you cannot see it. Deeper within the ruined stone, it grows heavier, more distant, colder. For a moment it becomes difficult to tell where the familiarity of your own power ends and where what acts upon it within the darkness begins.

You do not fight it. You do not try to drive the darkness from the image. You simply hold the connection.

Light emerges from a deep crack.

It is still your Thal’ithara.

But it is not entirely as it was when it entered.

Something dark lies within its glow—not like a foreign body, an illness or a corruption. Light and darkness remain distinct, yet for this moment belong to the same trace.
