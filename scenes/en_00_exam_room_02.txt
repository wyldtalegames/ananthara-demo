[b]THE GIFT OF LIGHT[/b]

Behind you, the deep rumble of the retreating wall falls silent. For several moments you hear only your own footsteps before the narrow passage opens into a second chamber. It is larger than the first and almost entirely empty. No scroll waits upon a lectern, and no row of symbols immediately demands your attention. Instead, warm white light falls through a narrow opening high above. It strikes a cut piece of glass or crystal held in a stone setting near the ceiling and is directed diagonally across the room. Where the beam is meant to lead is not immediately apparent, for almost the entire opposite wall is occupied by an enormous mosaic—and something is wrong with it.

The mural is not destroyed. No stone is missing; no loose fragments lie on the floor. Even so, it looks as though someone dismantled the whole work and reassembled it incorrectly. A painted hand ends at the edge of one segment and becomes a vine on the next. Golden lines break off in mid-motion and continue several metres away. Pieces of robes, faces, plants and dark fields appear where they cannot possibly belong. Only when you step closer do you see that the mosaic consists of immense stone segments. Fine seams divide them; some panels rest in circular guides, others in straight tracks that reach deep into the wall. The individual pieces can evidently be rotated or shifted.

This is not a ruined artwork. Someone deliberately took it out of order.

The final segment reaches its place. A deep sound travels through the wall, and the guides behind the mosaic engage. One by one, the movable stones sink back a few finger-widths until the seams lie almost flush. What had seemed like dozens of isolated fragments becomes a single image. You step back. For the first time, you see the mural as it was created.

Three great figures dominate its centre. A woman stands between two male figures, her hands open before her. Vines cross her robes and wind around her arms, and despite the stylized depiction, you recognize the motif. Faon’lin. One of the Three. In the stories, she is associated with growth, creation and the unfolding of life. Something bright floats between her open hands. Perhaps a flame, perhaps light, perhaps something for which the artist chose no more definite form. Golden lines extend outward from it. They reach people and animals, plants and trees, tools, buildings, and things that resemble works of art or crafted objects. Some lines do not end with a single figure. They continue, divide, meet one another and connect what has been created.

Yet the farther your gaze follows the image outward, the more it changes. Open hands become closed. Fingers curl around the same golden lines that once ran freely through the image. In some places they are drawn together; in others, pulled apart. And over it lies the black veil. It does not begin at any single point. Dark fields creep across the image from several directions, covering parts of the golden lines and swallowing shapes still faintly visible beneath them. Eyes lie within the darkness. Some are barely suggested; others are rendered clearly enough that their gaze is almost unpleasant. They do not look at you. They look inward—toward Faon’lin holding the light between her open hands.

It recalls the first section of the examination: gift, growth, connection, and something laid over them. But resemblance is not proof.

The first impression changes at once. Up close, there had been points of light, separate lines and crystals. With every step of distance, they begin to relate to one another. Even as you watch the book, the light begins to change. The first point goes dark, yet its glow does not vanish. It retreats along one of the fine lines to the next point. That one follows. Then the next. One by one, the points extinguish as their light runs backwards through the network. The open book slowly dissolves. Pages become lines, lines become points, points become individual veins of light.

The light withdraws from the mural’s outer reaches: from the places where the veil covers it, from created things, from connections between people and figures, from branches, leaves, trunk and roots. The three paths emerge more clearly as everything they revealed together fades. At last they too flow back towards the centre. Between Faon’lin’s open hands, the light gathers one final time.

Then a deep rumble passes through the chamber.

You step back. A fine vertical line appears in the mosaic. At first you think it is one of the old seams between the movable segments, but this line continues through figures, golden paths and the black veil. A second appears on the opposite side. Dust spills to the floor.

The entire mural moves. Not individual segments this time. The complete stone surface slides slowly backwards. Immense mechanisms engage behind it, and then the relief divides along the new seam. Its two halves move apart.

Cool air brushes your skin. Where the mural stood, a narrow passage now lies open. Beyond it waits another chamber.
