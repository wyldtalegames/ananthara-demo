The chamber is smaller than you expected. Cold stone surrounds you on every side. No window breaks the walls, and no torch lights the room. Only a weak glow enters through the narrow seams of the door behind you, fading after a few steps into darkness. In the center stands a stone lectern. A single scroll rests upon it.

“Find the secret of this ruin. When you believe you understand it, find your way out.” That is all you were given.

You step closer. The parchment is marked by age: dark edges, fine cracks and faded signs cross its surface. Some are familiar; others almost disappear into the parchment. Among them are curved characters you recognize. Têhand.

You reach for it. The moment your fingers touch the parchment, a faint tingling passes beneath your skin. Something answers. A fine shimmer travels beneath your fingertips and through the writing. It follows one sign, then the next, spreading like liquid light across the scroll. Individual characters flare gold for a heartbeat. The energy reaches the edge and disappears into the stone lectern.

A deep tone fills the chamber. You pull your hand away. For a moment everything is still. Then fire blooms behind you. One torch. A second answers across the room, then a third. One after another, flames awaken along the walls until warm light pushes the darkness back.

But the light reveals something else. Fine lines begin to glow where there was only bare stone. Symbols emerge from walls and floor as if they had slept beneath the surface: dozens of them, some no larger than your hand, others spread across several stone plates. You recognize a flame. A blade. Water. A crown. Something like a sun. Roots and growing vines. Lines that interlock. Farther back, a sign like an eye, half-hidden behind something resembling a veil.

You look back to the scroll. Its golden shimmer has weakened, but has not vanished completely. The scroll did not simply react to your touch. It reacted to you.

What do you investigate first?

  You step closer to the stone lectern and focus on the scroll.

The parchment is old, and its writing is only partly preserved. Some lines can be read without difficulty; from others, only individual words or characters remain. Between them are places where the ink has faded so completely that entire portions of the text are missing.

Part of it was written in ordinary script, and part in Têhand, that ancient language whose characters are still found in rituals, oaths, seals and traditions dating back to the age of the gods. Not every word in Têhand carries magic. Yet you have learned never to call unknown words harmless merely because you can translate them.

One of the surviving lines feels familiar—not because you have read this exact sentence before, but because its components belong to those you encountered repeatedly during your training.

[i]Don wuvurep dêkh posehkund sundaykh, elvip wuvurep dêkh posehkund duldaykh.[/i]

You work through the familiar characters in your mind. Not every linguistic nuance yields itself immediately, but the meaning is clear enough:

“What was hidden reveals itself not to the eye, but to the light.”

You read the translation again. Then you tilt the parchment. Torchlight skims across its surface. For a moment you think you see more signs between two faded passages.

You change the angle. They vanish.

The Têhand sentence suddenly sounds much less metaphorical.

What do you do?

This time, the final symbol does not fade.

Red. Green. Gold. Violet.

The four colours lie across the stone plates like parts of a single image. The Flame’s deep tone sounds again, followed by Growth’s brighter note. Connection’s two tones answer one another. At last, the muted sound of the Veil settles beneath them.

For one moment the separate tones exist beside one another. Then they become a chord.

Fine lines begin to glow in the floor. They run from one symbol to the next: Flame to Growth, Growth to Connection, and from there to Veil. What first seemed like four separate signs becomes one movement through the room.

Stone grinds. Dust spills from a seam in the opposite wall. You step back. A wall you had taken for solid slowly sinks into the floor.

Another chamber lies beyond.

Yet before you take your first step through, your gaze returns to the stone lectern. The scroll still lies there.
